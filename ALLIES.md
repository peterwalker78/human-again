# Code for allies

Human Again is open to other people who make software with AI and want it to be on people's side. Useful apps of every kind are welcome — a timer, a reader, a notebook, a game, a tool for a real job. The one bar is that your app is at least as innocent as ours.

A lot of software made with AI is made carelessly, and people are right to be wary of it. We'd rather answer that with a standard anyone can check than with an argument.

## The promise

These apps exist to help people be the best, kindest versions of themselves, and never to exploit them. The nine rules below serve that. Slipstream, Glimmerwood and Westering are the measure: the code allows what they do, and no more. Where the rules are silent or unclear, the promise decides, always for the person using the app.

## The nine

1. **On people's side.** The app is made for the people using it and never works against them or anyone else. It doesn't lie to, exploit or harm anyone. Any nudge is in the open, and towards what the person already wants. It watches no one but the person using it, and only after telling them plainly. No design tricks, no random rewards, nothing there only for a limited time, and no betting money, or anything bought with money, on chance.
2. **Honest.** Everything you say about the app, anywhere — the app itself, its page, its shop listing, the form to join — is true, and stays true. It does nothing people aren't told about, told where they'll see it before they use it. The README says plainly if AI helped make the app's code, words, art or music.
3. **Open, and paid for once at most.** Everything the app ships is public and something anyone can open and check: code under a licence approved by the Open Source Initiative, and art, sound, fonts, data and any model under an OSI or a recognised free-culture or open-data licence. The builds you ship are made from all of that in public, so anyone can check they match. The app can be free, ask for donations or have a one-off price. No subscriptions, adverts, paid add-ons, microtransactions or DLC.
4. **Nothing leaves the computer unless people ask.** The app sends nothing anywhere until the person asks it to or turns it on. That means no telemetry, analytics, crash reports, update checks or licence checks, and no fonts or scripts quietly loaded from elsewhere. Nobody needs an account. If a part of the app talks to a server, that server keeps this whole code too, and we check what it sends and what comes back. Extras people switch on are fine; turning the app on is not the same as turning that on.
5. **No AI features.** The app itself can be made with AI, and AI can help write its words, art or music — as long as a person reviewed them and they ship fixed, the same for everyone, there to be read. What the app must never do is make things for the person as they use it: nothing that writes, draws, speaks or answers for them on the fly, and nothing they type or make goes to an AI service. The one exception is getting at what's already there — reading the app's own text aloud, or turning speech into text — never inventing anything new.
6. **Nothing is counted against anyone.** Taking a break never costs anything, or lowers anything, however long it lasts. An app can show you a plain, gentle picture of your own days — even one that grows as the good days add up — so long as it only ever reflects, never randomises or rushes you, and a day off takes nothing away. The banned kind is the opposite: streaks, scores, badges or leaderboards that reward, rank or compare people, or that a break resets. If an app uses a little play to help with a habit the person chose, research that tested this kind of play must back it, and the README must name it. Games that are plainly games can use scores, levels and the rest — but no gambling, no random prizes and nothing there only for a limited time.
7. **Kindness over guilt.** The app never scolds, shames or guilt-trips — including making someone feel they let a character down, or framing a choice as a loss. It only reminds people of things they themselves switched on, and never keeps on at them, by any kind of alert. Limits people set for themselves are fine, and they can always change their minds.
8. **Built to be put down.** Nothing is designed to keep people looking for longer than they meant to. Lists end where they said they would. The app doesn't start playing anything the person didn't choose — though opening an app whose whole point is sound or motion is itself that choice, as long as stopping it is easy. Alerts are only about things people want to know, never to pull them back in.
9. **When in doubt, people come first.** If a rule could be read two ways, the reading that leaves people calmer and freer to put the app down wins — not the one that keeps them in it. This never loosens another rule, and nothing in this code is ever a reason to harm anyone. The badge speaks for the app alone: it can't be used to vouch for anything else its makers sell. And we never use this code to attack anyone, including apps that leave.

Everything the app ships is covered, including its content and what its libraries do. Pages a browser shows are the person's own to visit — what those pages do is the web, not the app — but anything the app itself reaches for, including any server it needs to work, is fully covered.

## The lock

This code only ever gets stricter, and only ever in people's favour. No change may weaken the promise, weaken this lock, or let in something a rule now keeps out — a change like that simply isn't this code, and can't carry the name or the mark. Every version is public and dated, so any attempt to loosen it is on the record. The last version that kept the promise is always the real one.

## Who can join

Useful apps of any kind are welcome. The bar is that your app is at least as innocent as ours, which in practice means:

- it runs on the person's own computer;
- it works without a server at its heart;
- it ships no AI that makes things for the person while they use it;
- every word, picture and sound in it is fixed, and anyone can open and check it.

If an app can't be checked this way — it needs a live server to work, or it makes its content on the fly — we can't vouch for it, however good it is, and we'll say so plainly. That's not a judgement on the app; it's the limit of what we can honestly stand behind.

## How we check

Before an app joins, we read everything it ships — code and content both — and run its latest release in a sandbox with no network, watching every file it opens and everything it tries to reach. We judge it by how it would behave on any day, at any time, after any amount of use, for anyone, anywhere — not only in the one run we did; we'll move the clock and the usage forward and try it as different people. Anything the app loads later, like a pack or a preset, is part of what we check, and default content ships in the source. We do the same for every release after that.

When a case is unclear, we decide in the open issue and say why.

Anyone can tell us about a broken promise, in an ally's app or one of ours, with the [Report a broken promise](https://github.com/peterwalker78/human-again/issues/new?template=broken-promise.yml) form.

We only take on as many allies as we can really check. A badge that meant "nobody had time" would be worth less than none.

## One strike

A release that breaks the code ends the alliance. There's no warning period. The app comes off the [allies page](https://peterwalker78.github.io/human-again/allies.html), and the page says which promise it broke, and nothing more. The decision is final.

A strike follows the maker, not just the app: a struck app can't rejoin under a new name, and joining means telling us about any app of yours that was struck before. Coming back needs a public account of what changed and a cooling-off period, decided in the open.

## What allies get

- A place on the [allies page](https://peterwalker78.github.io/human-again/allies.html), with a link and a line about what the app is for.
- The right to say their app keeps the Human Again code, with a link to it.

## Joining

Open a [Join as an ally](https://github.com/peterwalker78/human-again/issues/new?template=join-as-ally.yml) issue. Tell us what the app is for, where its source is, and how it's made. We'll answer there, and check it in the open, in the same issue.

## Changes

This file is the code, and the lock above governs how it may change. Every change is in its history, and allies are told in their issue before a change applies to them.
