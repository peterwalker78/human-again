# Code for allies

Human Again is open to other builders who make software with AI and want it to be on people's side. An ally's app keeps the same promises Slipstream, Glimmerwood and Westering keep, and we check that it does.

A lot of software made with AI is made carelessly, and people are right to be wary of it. We'd rather answer that with a standard anyone can check than with an argument.

## The code

1. **On the person's side.** The app is made to help the person using it, and is never built to harm, deceive, manipulate or exploit anyone: the person using it, the people around them, or anyone else. No dark patterns, no gambling mechanics, and nothing that watches anyone without their knowing.
2. **Honest.** The app does what it says and hides nothing, and its README says plainly that AI is used in writing its code.
3. **Free and open.** The full source is public under an open-source licence, and the builds you ship are made from that source in public, so anyone can check they match.
4. **Nothing leaves the computer unless the person asks.** No telemetry, analytics, crash reports, accounts or cloud by default, and no update checks unless the person turns them on. The only network traffic is what the person asked for.
5. **No AI features.** No model runs in the app, and nothing a person types or makes is sent to one. The code itself can be written by AI.
6. **Nothing is counted against anyone.** No streaks, scores, badges or leaderboards. Missing a day costs nothing.
7. **Kindness over guilt.** Nothing scolds, nags or shames, and every choice about a person's own time stays with them.
8. **Built to be put down.** No endless feeds, autoplay, or notifications meant to bring people back. Nothing is designed to keep someone looking for longer than they meant to.
9. **Read kindly, never as an excuse.** Where a promise could be read two ways, the reading that's better for the person using the app wins. No promise is ever a reason to harm anyone, and keeping the code vouches for the app, not for anything else its makers do.

The code covers everything an app ships, including what its libraries do.

## How we check

Before an app joins, we read its source and run its latest release in a sandbox with no network, recording every file it opens and everything it tries to reach. We check every release after that in the same way.

Anyone can tell us about a broken promise with the [Report a broken promise](https://github.com/peterwalker78/human-again/issues/new?template=broken-promise.yml) form.

## One strike

A release that breaks the code ends the alliance. There's no warning period: the app comes off the [allies page](https://peterwalker78.github.io/human-again/allies.html), and the page says which promise it broke, and nothing more. The decision is final.

## What allies get

- A place on the [allies page](https://peterwalker78.github.io/human-again/allies.html), with a link and a line about what the app is for.
- The right to say their app keeps the Human Again code, with a link to it.

## Joining

Open a [Join as an ally](https://github.com/peterwalker78/human-again/issues/new?template=join-as-ally.yml) issue. Tell us what the app is for, where its source is, and how it's made. We'll answer there, and the check happens in the open, in that issue.
